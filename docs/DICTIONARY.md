# Dictionary build

`server/dictionary.json` is generated by `server/build_dictionary.py` from:

| Source | Licence | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ECDICT | MIT | words, Chinese and English meanings, British transcription, exam tags, frequency |
| Tatoeba (via manythings.org) | CC BY 2.0 FR | example sentences; evidence for which Chinese gloss is common |
| WordNet 3.0 `index.sense` | WordNet licence (Princeton, permissive) | SemCor counts: which part of speech is used most |
| CMU Pronouncing Dictionary | BSD-2-Clause | General American pronunciation |

Notices: [NOTICE.md](../NOTICE.md). How to run the build:
[STARTUP.md](STARTUP.md#重新生成词典). The same inputs always give the same
file (checked by a test that runs the build under different hash seeds).

Each entry has: `word`, `phonetic` (General American IPA), `phoneticUk`
(ECDICT), `meaning` (the core meaning for the card), `translation` (all
ECDICT lines, core line first), `definition` (English), `level`, `tags`,
`labels`, `example`, `exampleZh`, and two build notes kept only in the JSON:
`meaningSource` and `phoneticSource`.

## Which words are included

A word is included when ECDICT has a usable Chinese meaning for it and at
least one of these holds:

| Rule | Evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Existing rule | Lowercase lemma with an exam tag (中考 … 托福). Every word chosen this way before Phase 3A is still chosen. |
| Exam tag | Exam-tagged words the old rule dropped by mistake (see below). |
| Core | Oxford core flag or 3+ Collins stars in ECDICT. |
| Frequency | COCA rank ≤ 10,000, or BNC rank ≤ 10,000 with at least one Collins star. |
| Override | Listed in `dictionary_overrides.json` with a reason. |

Fixes to the old rule:

- **Forms that are words in their own right.** ECDICT links `meeting`,
  `interested`, `according` and `bacteria` to a base word. They are now
  included when they have a non-verb meaning of their own. Plain past tenses,
  regular comparatives (`cheaper`) and plurals of an included word
  (`investigators`) stay out.
- **False inflections.** A word with inflections of its own is a lemma, even
  when ECDICT also lists it as a form of another word: `number` (numbers,
  numbered) is not just the comparative of `numb`.
- **Domain-only meanings.** Words whose ECDICT meaning only has domain lines
  (`[计] 因特网`) keep those lines, with the part of speech of their English
  definition. For these words an exam tag alone is not enough, because ECDICT
  also tags fragments and names such as `attent` and `cance`.
- **No cut meanings.** Every part of speech is kept. Before, only 4 lines were
  kept, and words such as `like` lost their preposition meaning.

Left out: names (`约翰（男子名）`), words without a vowel (`km`), entries that
are only abbreviations or only filler interjections (`uh`, `hmm`), and new
British/American spelling variants of an included word with the same meaning.
The variant check never removes a word that was already in the dictionary.
Capitalised words (`I`, `Monday`, `English`) are only added through overrides.

## Overrides

`server/dictionary_overrides.json` is versioned with the code and applied after
the rules on every build, so corrections survive rebuilds.

- `include` / `exclude`: single words, each with a reason. An `exclude` with
  `kind: needs_verified_meaning` marks a word we want, but whose ECDICT meaning
  is wrong or outdated (`download`, `online`, `stakeholder`). Its meaning must
  come from a verified source, never written ad hoc.
- `translation_keep_lines` / `phonetic`: field fixes. Each stores the source
  value it was written against. If ECDICT changes that value, the fix is
  reported in `dictionary.build.json` under `problems` instead of applied.

## Core meaning

`server/meanings.py` picks one part of speech and up to three glosses that
ECDICT already lists:

- **Part of speech.** Two signals decide:
  - SemCor tag counts from WordNet: how often each part of speech was used.
  - Tatoeba: how often a gloss that belongs to only one part of speech appears
    in the human Chinese translation of sentences containing the word.

  Limits on each signal:
  - SemCor is 1960s written English and leans towards verbs. WordNet alone
    therefore only replaces ECDICT's first line when that line has at most a
    quarter of the tagged uses, and never replaces a function-word line.
  - WordNet has no prepositions, so its adverb tags for words like *within*
    point to the preposition line.
  - Tatoeba overrules WordNet only where WordNet has no tagged use of that
    part of speech (*please*: adv. 请), and never for adjectives (Chinese
    drops 的).
- **Glosses.** Glosses confirmed by Tatoeba come first; otherwise the first two
  in ECDICT order. Matches inside a longer gloss of another part of speech do
  not count. One-character glosses (产) may not jump ahead of an earlier gloss.
- **Overrides.** `meaning` entries in `dictionary_overrides.json` reorder
  ECDICT's own glosses, with a reason. The build rejects a gloss the word does
  not have. Replacing a wrong ECDICT meaning needs at least two independent
  sources, listed in the entry (*download*, *stakeholder* …).

`meaningSource` records which rule decided. In the build of 2026-10-24:

| Source | Words |
| --- | --- |
| Only one part of speech | 8,985 |
| WordNet and/or Tatoeba | 1,955 |
| No evidence (ECDICT order kept) | 1,967 |
| Override | 36 |

The words with no evidence are where most remaining errors are (see the
evaluation in the Phase 3B report).

## Usage labels

`labels` holds space-separated usage labels from overrides:

| Label | Meaning | Example | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `vulgar` | taboo swear word | *fuck* | studied only after opting in |
| `offensive` | main use is an insult or slur | *negro* | studied only after opting in |
| `offensive-sense` | ordinary main meaning, but also offensive | *ass* | none |
| `mild` | mild swearing | *damn* | none |

Words labelled `vulgar` or `offensive` stay in the dictionary and search, but
new study words skip them unless the setting `includeSensitive` is on.

## Pronunciation

`phonetic` is General American IPA from CMUdict (`server/pronunciation.py`),
in learner-dictionary style:

- long vowels are marked with `ː`, and `ɝː`/`ɚ` are the r-coloured vowels;
- stress marks go before the syllable, which is split by maximal onset;
- one-syllable words get no stress mark;
- when CMUdict marks two primary stresses, only the last one stays primary.

Variants:

- Variants that only reduce a vowel, or differ only by the US cot–caught merger
  (*law*), are not shown.
- Stress-shift pairs follow the core part of speech: *record* n. /ˈrekɚd/,
  v. /rɪˈkɔːrd/.
- Vowel-changing heteronyms (*live*, *lead*, *tear*) are resolved by curated
  `pronunciation` overrides that must match a CMUdict variant.
- A few CMUdict stress errors (*unite*, *retort*) are corrected by overrides
  that cite their evidence.

Words missing from CMUdict are looked up through:

- a spelling variant (*colourful* → *colorful*);
- a regular suffix (*-ness*, *-less*, *-ful*, *-ly*);
- a compound of two known words of four or more letters. A compound is only
  used when ECDICT's transcription has the same syllable count and the stress
  on the first part.

Where all of these fail, `phonetic` falls back to the British transcription and
`phoneticSource` says `ecdict-uk` (290 words); 11 words have no pronunciation.

`phoneticUk` keeps ECDICT's British transcription in its own older style
(`ei`, `əu`, `əː`). The build only fixes characters; the transcription itself
is unchanged and has not been checked:

- ASCII marks become IPA (`'` `,` `.` → `ˈ` `ˌ`; `:` → `ː`).
- ECDICT's `ɒ:` becomes `ɔː`.
- Look-alike Cyrillic and Greek letters become `e` and `ə`.
- Known encoding damage is repaired (`\\:`, `^`).
- Variants are separated by `; `.

Choosing one modern IPA standard is a separate decision.

## Examples

Examples come from Tatoeba. With `--previous`, a word keeps the example it
already had. Next come reviewed examples from `tatoeba_examples.json` (see
below). Other new words get a Tatoeba example when `cmn.txt` is supplied, and
none otherwise.

### Candidates from the official Tatoeba export

`cmn.txt` (manythings.org) is a filtered subset of Tatoeba, about 32,000
pairs. The official export has about 78,000 English–Chinese pairs.
`server/tatoebaExamples.js` turns it into a **review sheet** for words that
have no example yet. It changes nothing else.

```bash
# inside server/; the files come from https://downloads.tatoeba.org/exports/
#   per_language/eng/eng_sentences_detailed.tsv.bz2
#   per_language/cmn/cmn_sentences_detailed.tsv.bz2
#   per_language/cmn/cmn-eng_links.tsv.bz2
#   user_languages.tar.bz2 (optional: native-speaker column)
cd .dict-src/tatoeba-official && bunzip2 -k *.bz2 && cd ../..
node tatoebaExamples.js --data .dict-src/tatoeba-official --book gk   # --sample 100 --seed 20261025
```

The output goes to `.ai-work/tatoeba/` (not committed):
- `<book>-candidates.tsv`: one row per word, the best pair;
- `<book>-sample100.tsv`: a reproducible sample;
- `<book>-summary.json`: counts and input hashes.

The first ten columns are the AI review columns (`decision` and so on). The
others keep the source: `eng_id`, `cmn_id`, `eng_author`, `cmn_author`,
`license` (CC BY 2.0 FR), plus review hints.

**Which words.** The same rule as AI examples: no human example, no accepted
AI example, not held, a verified core meaning, not vulgar or offensive.

**Which pairs.**
- The English contains the word or a regular form of it.
- The English passes the AI sentence checks and has 5–14 words.
- The Chinese is Simplified: every character is in GB2312, the same rule as
  the build.
- The sentence is not already an example anywhere, and is offered for one
  word only.

**Order.** For each word, pairs are ordered by:
1. the core gloss is visible in the Chinese;
2. no sensitive-topic words and no unknown names (real people?);
3. fewer words above the book's level;
4. native speakers;
5. a length near 9 words.

**`match` is a hint, not a check.**
- `form`: only the word form matched.
- `form+gloss`: the Chinese also contains a core gloss.

Neither proves the sentence uses the word in the target sense. Every row is
`needs_review`.

**Accepting.** Fill in `decision` (accept | edit | reject; corrections go in
`sentence_fix` / `translation_fix`), then run:

```bash
node tatoebaExamples.js --accept .ai-work/tatoeba/gk-review.tsv --reviewer NAME --data .dict-src/tatoeba-official
```

For every kept row, it checks again that:
- the ids still point to the same Tatoeba sentences;
- the word still has no human or AI example;
- the (possibly edited) text passes the checks.

Any error means nothing is written. Accepted rows go to
`server/tatoeba_examples.json` (committed), with ids, authors, licence, and
the original text of edited rows.

**Build.** `build_dictionary.py` reads `tatoeba_examples.json` by default
(`--examples` to choose another file). A reviewed example fills a word that
has no example yet; it never replaces one. The database picks the change up
on the next start, like any `dictionary.json` change.

| Book | Reviewed | Accepted | Edited | Rejected |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 高考 (2026-10-25) | 464 | 258 | 33 | 173 |

The filled-in review sheet, with every decision and reason, is kept in
`server/tatoeba_reviews/gk-2026-10-25.tsv`. Rejected pairs stay there. Doubtful core meanings found
during the review are listed in [MEANING_FLAGS.md](MEANING_FLAGS.md).

## Coverage counting

`server/dictionary_audit.py` produces every coverage number.

- **Reference.** ECDICT's `frq` column (a COCA word-form rank). `bnc` is
  reported the same way.
- **Band.** "Top N" means all ECDICT headwords with rank 1..N. Ranks count word
  forms, so a band mixes four classes:
  - lemmas (`number`)
  - forms of another lemma (`meetings`)
  - capitalised words (`English`)
  - multi-word entries
- **Lemma coverage** is the share of lemmas in the band that are in the
  dictionary.
- **Form reach** is the share of forms whose own entry or base lemma is in the
  dictionary.
- **Word families.** None of these numbers count families
  (`abandon`/`abandonment`/`abandoned`); that would need a family list.

Phase 2 reported 83% for the COCA top 10,000. That figure used the old lemma
rule, so `number` counted as a form. The method above gives 81.7% for the same
dictionary. Phase 2's "2,041 missing" counted every headword in the band,
including forms and capitalised words. Both numbers describe the same build.
